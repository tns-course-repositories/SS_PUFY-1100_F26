Material Matters

  • Materials are designed choices, not neutral substances.

  • Everyday objects connect to larger systems.

  • Why do we make things from the materials we do?

Source: (left) Prefabricated panel construction in 1961 and 1964, Darnytsia neighborhood, Kyiv, Ukraine. (right) Lustron House, USA, prior to installation, circa 1950.

The Everyday Object

  • A disposable spoon appears simple.

  • Its material reflects numerous design decisions.

  • Its useful life may be measured in minutes.

Take → Make → Waste (Linear)

  • Resources are extracted and processed.

  • Products circulate through consumption.

  • Material value is often lost after use.

Source: RIT

Rethinking Design

  • Waste begins with design decisions.

  • Circular systems keep products and materials in use.

  • Design can regenerate rather than deplete.

Source: RIT

What Is It Made Of?

  • Identify base materials, binders, coatings, and additives.

  • Distinguish natural origins from processed products.

  • Ingredient transparency is the starting point for evaluation.

Source: LVP Defined

Material Health Lens

  • Material health involves multiple interconnected factors.

  • Chemical hazards and ingredient disclosure are central.

  • Evaluation must extend beyond the finished product.

Source: Parsons Healthy Materials

Life-Cycle Impacts

  • Impacts occur during sourcing and manufacturing.

  • Transportation, use, and maintenance also matter.

  • Disposal, reuse, and recycling shape long-term consequences.

Source: Parsons Healthy Materials

Health + Exposure

  • Hazard and exposure are different considerations.

  • Workers, occupants, and communities may face different risks.

  • Material disclosure helps identify potential hazards.

Source: Blue Ridge Consultancy

Material Properties

  • Physical characteristics determine possible applications.

  • Performance involves trade-offs between properties.

  • Testing reveals behavior, not chemical safety.

The Hidden Cost

  • Environmental costs are not always reflected in price.

  • Extraction and manufacturing can displace impacts geographically.

  • Material decisions connect to environmental justice.

Source: Fifth Ward Elementary School and residential neighborhoods sit near the Denka Performance Elastomer Plant, back, in Reserve, La.

Material Substitution

  • Alternatives must satisfy functional requirements.

  • Renewable does not automatically mean healthier.

  • Every substitution introduces new trade-offs.

Source: Flooring Tech

Alternative Uses

  • Material properties can suggest unexpected applications.

  • Reuse challenges assumptions about intended function.

  • New uses require renewed performance and health evaluation.

Source: Superuse Studios - Speeltuin Wikado, Rotterdam

Materials In Art

  • Artists can reveal overlooked material characteristics.

  • Industrial and discarded materials can become artistic media.

  • Experimentation can challenge conventional material hierarchies.

Material Investigation

  • Observe physical characteristics and performance.

  • Investigate composition, production, health, and environmental impacts.

  • Evaluate and propose an alternative use.
