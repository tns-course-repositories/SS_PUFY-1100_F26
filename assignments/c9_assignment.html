🧭 Assignment 9 — Comparative Material Investigation

💡 Introduction

Class 9 — Thursday | 10-22-26 — begins with a visit to the Parsons Healthy Materials Lab and a lecture on the considerations involved in material analysis.

Through a two-part comparative investigation, you will examine materials used in art, design, construction, and everyday life. First, you will work with a small group to investigate physical samples in class; then, you will independently apply the same process to a material of your own choosing.

The goal is to observe, investigate, evaluate, and connect material properties to broader systems of production, use, human and environmental health, and disposal.

🧪 PART I — IN-CLASS GROUP MATERIAL INVESTIGATION

Thursday | 10-22-26 | In class

Work in a group of 3–4 students. Select one or two materials from the building and household material samples provided in class. Use the Healthy Materials Lab visit and Lecture 9 to guide your investigation.

01 — MATERIAL + USE

What is the material, and what is it used for?

Identify the sample, its likely composition, and its common uses in art, design, buildings, or everyday products. Note anything you cannot verify from the sample alone.

02 — PROPERTIES + PERFORMANCE

What can you learn by examining and testing the material?

Observe its texture, weight, flexibility, strength, surface, and response to simple classroom tests where appropriate. Record what you actually observe rather than assuming how it performs over time.

03 — PRODUCTION + SYSTEMS

Where does this material come from, and what does it take to produce?

Consider its raw materials, processing, energy inputs, transportation, use, and potential end-of-life pathways. Identify questions that would require additional research.

04 — HEALTH + ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACTS

What might make this material more or less desirable?

Consider possible chemical additives, human exposure, environmental impacts, durability, and potential alternatives. Distinguish documented facts from questions or hypotheses; physical inspection alone cannot establish material safety.

05 — EVALUATION + ALTERNATIVE USE SCENARIOS

What are its trade-offs, and how else could it be used?

Briefly evaluate the material’s advantages, limitations, and unknowns. Then propose a use beyond its typical application, drawing on observed physical, structural, or aesthetic characteristics. Explain what makes that use plausible and what new limitations or health and environmental questions it raises.

06 — GROUP DISCUSSION

What did your group find?

Share a brief summary with the class: the material’s identity and uses, key physical observations, possible health and environmental considerations, one alternative use scenario, and one important unanswered question. If you selected two materials, highlight their most meaningful differences.

📝 PART II — INDEPENDENT MATERIAL INVESTIGATION

Complete individually outside of class.

Choose one material of your own used in art making, design, construction, or everyday life. Repeat the investigation process introduced in Part I, now using independent research to support and extend your observations.

01 — MATERIAL + USE

Identify your material, its composition, and its typical applications. Explain why you selected it.

02 — PROPERTIES + PERFORMANCE

Document its observable or researched physical characteristics and functional advantages or limitations. Clearly distinguish your own observations from information obtained from sources.

03 — PRODUCTION + SYSTEMS

Trace its primary raw materials and production process. Consider resource extraction or cultivation, manufacturing, energy, transportation, and end-of-life possibilities.

04 — HEALTH + ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACTS

Investigate relevant human-health and environmental concerns. Look for credible material or manufacturer information and acknowledge missing information or uncertainty.

05 — EVALUATION + ALTERNATIVE USE SCENARIOS

What are the material’s trade-offs, and how might it be used differently?

Evaluate its advantages and limitations for its intended use, weighing performance against production, health, and environmental implications. Then propose a new application in art, design, construction, or everyday life. Explain which physical, structural, or aesthetic characteristics support your proposal, what limitations might arise, and whether this new use introduces additional health or environmental considerations.

06 — CONCLUSION + REFLECTION

What has this investigation changed about your understanding of the material?

Summarize what you can substantiate, what remains unknown, and whether you would recommend the material for its intended or proposed alternative use. Identify one question that would require further investigation.

As in the Class 7 assignment, document your investigation through 300–400 words, 1–2 photographs or relevant images, and specific observations. Include links or citations to the sources used for factual claims about composition, production, and impacts.
