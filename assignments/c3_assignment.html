🧭 Assignment 03 - Making Systems Legible

💡 Introduction

Climate systems are difficult to perceive directly. They operate across scales, through invisible processes, between interconnected systems, and across periods of time that exceed ordinary human experience. Throughout Class 3, we examined how scientific and graphic representations make these relationships visible.

This week’s assignment challenge is not simply to show information. It is to decide:

What does the system do, and what graphic operation can make that behavior legible?

In this assignment you will work with one climate-system relationship and develop a visual strategy for communicating its behavior.

The assignment has two parts:

PART I — EXPERIMENT (In Class)
Develop and test multiple ways of representing the relationship during class.

PART II — DEVELOP (Outside Class- Upcoming Week)
Select one experiment and develop it into a resolved physical drawing outside class.

✏️ PART I — IN CLASS

50 minutes Total Time

DEFINE → STUDY → EXPERIMENT → TEST → SELECT

The goal of Part I is not to make a finished drawing.

The goal is to test multiple ways of making the same climate-system relationship visible.

01 — DEFINE

5 minutes

Choose one climate-system relationship:

RELATIONSHIP SYSTEM BEHAVIOR
Emissions → atmospheric CO₂ ACCUMULATION
Warming → ice loss → lower albedo → additional warming FEEDBACK
Stable condition → threshold → altered state THRESHOLD
Geological carbon → extraction → combustion → atmosphere TIME / ACCELERATION
Cause now → consequence later LAG
Atmosphere ↔︎ ocean ↔︎ land ↔︎ biosphere INTERDEPENDENCE
Local energy → infrastructure → atmosphere → Earth system SCALE / CONNECTION

In one sentence, describe what is happening in your system:

In this system: ______________________________________

02 — STUDY

7 minutes

Open the Class 3 Demo — System Behavior → Graphic Behavior.

Find two precedents that could help you think about your system behavior.

Do not ask:

What does this example look like?

Ask:

What does this example do?

Identify the graphic operations being used:

line · point · shape · field · position · scale · value · density · repetition · sequence · overlap · color

Use the examples as tools, not templates.

Note

By the end of this study, you should have identified several graphic operations that could make your system behavior visible. These become the starting points for your three experiments.

03 — EXPERIMENT

25 minutes

Produce three different representations of the same climate-system relationship.

the phenomenon stays the same.

the representation changes.

Each experiment should test a different dominant graphic operation or combination of operations.

For example:

ACCUMULATION

→ repetition + density

→ position + scale

→ sequence + value

Work quickly.

These are experiments, not finished drawings.

The objective is to discover which graphic strategy makes the relationship most legible.

04 — TEST

8 minutes

Work with a partner.

Before explaining your drawings, ask your partner to look at all three experiments.

Your partner should identify:

  • What relationship appears to be happening?
  • What do they understand first?
  • Where does the representation become confusing?
  • Which experiment is the most legible?

Then explain the relationship you intended to communicate.

Compare their reading with your intention.

05 — SELECT

5 minutes

Choose one experiment to develop for Part II.

Record:

RELATIONSHIP — What are you communicating?

BEHAVIOR — What does the system do?

OPERATION — What graphic strategy are you using?

NEXT MOVE — What needs to change to make the relationship more legible?

🔧 PART II — OUTSIDE CLASS

01 DEVELOP

Take the strongest experiment from Part I and develop it into a resolved physical drawing.

You are not starting over.

Part II asks you to refine, clarify, edit, and resolve the graphic strategy you developed during class.

The phenomenon remains the same.

The representation becomes more deliberate.

02 OBJECTIVE

The goal is not to produce the most complicated representation.

The goal is to make a difficult relationship legible.

Your final work should demonstrate a clear connection between:

SYSTEM BEHAVIOR → GRAPHIC BEHAVIOR

> THE DESIGN PROBLEM

Your final drawing should allow a viewer to understand an important behavior or relationship within a climate system.

As you develop the drawing, consider the following design variables that apply to your work:

> HIERARCHY

What should the viewer perceive first?

> RELATIONSHIP

What needs to appear connected?

> SEQUENCE

Does the viewer need to understand an order or progression?

> SCALE

Can differences in size communicate differences in magnitude, duration, or importance?

> VALUE / DENSITY

Can intensity communicate accumulation, change, or difference?

> REDUCTION

What information can be removed without losing meaning?

> LABELING

What information (if any) must be named for the system to remain understandable?

> COLOR

If you use color, what information does it communicate?

> MATERIAL + FORMAT

The strong suggestion is to use Bristol paper as noted in the syllabus.

This is a physical drawing assignment.

You may work with:

  • graphite
  • ink
  • marker
  • colored pencil
  • other drawing media available to you

Color is optional.

Your solution may be analytical, abstract, sequential, or a hybrid.

Represent the Behavior - Not simply the Object.

03 DOCUMENTATION

  • be evenly lit
  • avoid strong shadows or glare
  • crop out unnecessary surroundings
