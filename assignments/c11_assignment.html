🧭 Assignment 11 — Material Afterlives: From Disposability to Transformation

💡 Introduction

Class 11 — Thursday | 11-05-26 — begins with a virtual tour of the Sunset Park Material Recovery Facility, where discarded objects are sorted and processed as recoverable materials. In the lab, we will take a different approach: investigate how the material, manufactured form, and original function of disposable objects can become starting points for unexpected artistic and spatial transformations.

This is not a challenge to make a useful product from trash. Rather than deciding what to make in advance, begin with careful observation and let the material’s behavior and your experiments guide the outcome. The result may be sculptural, pictorial, architectural, structural, or difficult to categorize.

Process: EXAMINE → TRANSFORM → RECOMPOSE → SHARE

🔎 IN-CLASS GROUP LAB — MATERIAL AFTERLIVES

Thursday | 11-05-26 | In class

Work in your usual groups of 3–4 students. Use the collection of provided clean, disposable and cast-off materials. You may combine materials and use the basic tools and joining supplies made available in class. Handle sharp edges and tools carefully; ask before using any unfamiliar material or process. Use provided gloves with the materials as needed.

The objective is an investigation through making, not a polished or functional finished object. Keep evidence of your experiments so your group can explain how its thinking changed.

1 — EXAMINE

What was this material designed to do, and how does its form make that possible?

Select one or more materials from the shared collection. Examine their shape, surface, thickness, rigidity, flexibility, color, connections, and manufacturing features. Consider how the original form supports an intended function—and what happens when that function is no longer needed.

Make a quick sketch or take a photograph. Identify one formal or material characteristic your group wants to test.

2 — TRANSFORM

What can you discover by changing the material rather than designing toward a predetermined result?

Test at least two distinct operations. You might cut, fold, flatten, bend, repeat, stack, connect, suspend, reposition, color, or combine. Draw on the artists introduced in the lecture, but do not reproduce their artworks.

Notice what changes: structure, behavior, texture, scale, visual identity, or spatial relationships. If an experiment fails or produces something unexpected, use that discovery to guide the next action. Photograph or retain evidence of at least two tests.

3 — RECOMPOSE

What unexpected form, relationship, or experience can emerge from your experiments?

Select the most promising discovery and develop it further as a temporary composition, material study, assemblage, surface, or spatial construction. You may introduce color, repetition, or new connections if these deepen the investigation.

Do not worry about making a conventional object or giving the work a practical function. Be prepared to identify what changed between the original disposable object and your final configuration.

4 — GROUP PRESENTATIONS + DISCUSSION

What did your group discover?

Each group will give a brief 3–4 minute presentation showing:

  • ORIGINAL FORM: What material or object did you start with, and what was it designed to do?
  • OPERATIONS: Which transformations did you test, and what did you learn from them?
  • NEW POSSIBILITY: What emerged that you did not anticipate?
  • SYSTEMS QUESTION: Did your intervention change the material’s potential for reuse or recovery—or primarily change its artistic meaning? Explain the distinction.

Share your process as well as your outcome. A compelling experiment does not have to make the material more recyclable; artistic transformation and material circularity are different questions.

📖 READINGS — Class 11: Material Afterlives

1. Tara Donovan — Hammer Projects: Tara Donovan

Paul Brewer | Hammer Museum, 2004

Read the essay

This essay explores how Tara Donovan transforms ordinary manufactured materials into unexpected sculptural forms through repetition, accumulation, and experimentation. Her work demonstrates how the inherent properties of materials can guide the creative process, producing complex structures that often resemble natural systems.

Consider: How can repeated, simple actions transform familiar materials into something entirely unexpected?

2. El Anatsui — Ideas and Creativity Are Not Restricted to Any One Place

Art Fund, 2023

Read the interview

El Anatsui discusses his use of unconventional and discarded materials, including bottle caps, to create intricate sculptural surfaces. His practice reveals how an object’s physical characteristics, cultural history, and previous function can become the starting points for new artistic possibilities.

Consider: How can transforming a discarded material change not only its physical form, but also its identity and meaning?
